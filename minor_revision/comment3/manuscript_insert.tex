\paragraph{Information retention under edge selection.}
We compare fuzzy retrieval with alternative subgraph selectors while fixing
candidate order and matching the unique-edge budget for each query. Candidate
incidence coverage, query--candidate reachability, and exact path-witness
retention are assessed separately. Although fuzzy retrieval preserves all
source-covered candidates on the five evaluated datasets, its retention of
query--candidate reachability within three edges ranges from 0.4835 to 0.9546.
It retains more reachability than uniform random selection on PharmKG, DRKG,
and repoDB, whereas the PrimeKG and Hetionet estimates are similar to random.
A shortest-path baseline retains nearly all source-reachable candidates at
the same edge count, but sacrifices incidence coverage for other candidates.
These findings indicate a coverage-oriented compression trade-off rather than
lossless information preservation. Supplementary experiments provide
five-seed comparisons, budget curves, explicit source denominators, a
decomposition of duplicate versus unique-edge removal, and frozen-model
representation and generated-answer diagnostics. Paths are structural
witnesses and are not interpreted as validated biomedical mechanisms.
